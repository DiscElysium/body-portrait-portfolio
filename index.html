Four tennis players described different reasons to keep and edit their footage. Those differences changed who makes the creative decisions in the film workflow.

Project source

Information Visualization 2026, Tongji University College of Design and Innovation, with Alibaba’s Orange Lion Sports. A three-person course project. The certificate names the Best Creation Award.

My contribution

I introduced player intent into the workflow, designed the choice system, worked on motion analysis and effects, and designed the tabletop interaction and 16 portrait cards.

I01 / Recording type

Keeps footage mainly for private replay. The design makes private archiving available. Interview excerpt E01, 03:21–03:43.

I04 / Reflective type

Wants to decide which moments are worth keeping. The director crew prototype gives the player a clip confirmation step. Interview excerpt E03, 02:21–02:44.

I03 / Growth type

Wants the film to convey their attitude and the feeling of play. The design lets players set the tone. Interview excerpt E02, 05:33–05:46.

I02 / Expressive type

Worries a public post of poor play could invite criticism. The design lets players choose who sees the film. Interview 04:36–04:57.

02 Choices

Enlarge ↗
Read this page

I proposed bringing the player’s intent into a workflow first planned as fully automatic, then designed four choices to do it.

From interviews to design

Private replay informed the question about purpose and the option to archive. The desire to show how play felt informed the choice between technique and atmosphere. A wish to select highlights informed the option to choose clips or delegate.

Four choices

Motivation: for myself or for social media. Focus: technique or atmosphere. Data use: record and archive or visual expression. Social mode: reserved or proactive.

How the paths work

Questions 1 and 3 determine the portrait group. Questions 2 and 4 vary the expression within it. The paths are grouped into Expressive (01–04), Growth (05–08), Reflective (09–12) and Recording (13–16).

Path 13 / Recording

For myself → Atmosphere → Record → Proactive. Card 13 suggests jump cuts, light editing and ambient sound. The physical interaction on page 4 shows how to wind this path.

Qualitative journey / six stages

Start playing, experience a highlight, review the footage, try editing, settle or stop, then save or share. Each stage has one interview-based difficulty and one intended prototype response. The two curves share the first two stages. Without the prototype, effort and uncertainty increase after review; settling or abandoning the edit is the lowest point. The prototype is intended to let players confirm clips, shape the tone, preview and revise, then choose an audience. Curve heights are illustrative. The pale yellow-green fill highlights the space between the two curves from review onward.

The gap at each stage

Review: scan long clips and risk missing a rally, or confirm the clips AI suggests. Editing: make manual fixes, or set a tone with the director crew. Completion: settle or stop, or preview and revise. Sharing: hesitate over public judgement, or save privately and choose who sees the film.

03 AI crew

Read this page

Six AI roles make the pipeline visible. Four player decisions shape what the film keeps, says and shares.

Six AI roles

Pose analyst tracks 33 body landmarks and supplies posture data. Ball scout reads ball and shot data for the player to check. Highlight scout finds candidate moments and proposes clips. Scriptwriter proposes a storyboard, captions and tone. VFX artist applies the chosen treatment. Editor combines shots, effects and music.

Four player decisions

Verify the reading: correct a reading before using it. Choose the moments: keep, add or delegate the clip selection. Set the tone: confirm a tone or skip the decision. Choose who sees it: preview, save privately or share.

Production pipeline

Outlined numbers refer to the six AI roles. Pose analyst and Ball scout provide pose and shot data. Highlight scout proposes candidate highlights. Scriptwriter, VFX artist and Editor work on film production. Editor delivers the film and report; the player decides the audience.

My motion pipeline

The source, person cutout and pose use the same source frame at 00:10. The mask isolates the player and the landmarks capture the pose. I implemented segmentation, pose analysis and timed effects.

04 Physical

▷ Explore all five filmsEnlarge ↗
Read this page

I made the choice system tangible. Follow a path, find its portrait card and try the treatment at the same table.

01 / Wind a path

Pull the thread from its hole. In each column, wind it around the post that matches how you want to express yourself.

02 / Match your card

Match the line to one of 16 cards. Card 13 suggests jump cuts, light edits and ambient sound. The illustrated route is For myself, Atmosphere, Record, Proactive.

03 / Try the prototype

Continue on the inset iPad. Try the director crew, review the options and compare the example film with your intention.

04 / Check the effect

Stack or swap clear effects over a fixed Federer image. Two locating pins keep the layers aligned as you compare treatments.

Two kinds of feedback

A film example on screen and a visual layer you can swap by hand give visitors two ways to judge whether the treatment feels right.

Beneath the printed surface

A small eyelet feeds the thread from below. The wood seats the copper posts and recesses the iPad. The charging cable runs beneath the printed cloth.

Explaining the stand in person

I guided visitors through the project, connecting the poster and screen to the tabletop interaction.

Key visual poster

Inspired by Leonardo da Vinci's Vitruvian Man, the poster reframes the body through a circular frame, pose landmarks and swing trajectories. Condensed orange and black type turns movement into a graphic portrait.

Five promotional films

Each film gives a different player's movement its own visual treatment, from comic effects to animated typography. Original stills from films 01, 02 and 05 are shown.

05 Exhibition

Enlarge ↗
Read this page

An exhibition that visitors could understand, choose, try and watch.

My exhibition work

I was responsible for the exhibition and interaction apparatus, bringing research panels, the film screen, winding area and portrait cards together as one stand.

Participation sequence

Read: find the project question. Choose: wind a path and find a portrait. Try: compare transparent effects. Watch: see the film possibilities.

Team recognition

An original exhibition award photograph appears at lower left. The central course exhibition group photograph marks my position at my feet.

Reflection

I helped bring personal ideas into generation, implemented underlying techniques and turned the choice system into a participatory physical experience.

Design interest

This clarified my design interest: connecting personal expression, AI-assisted creation and interaction so technology responds to a particular person.

Material language

White surfaces, orange headings and black type extended the project's visual language. Hanging body models, lines and cards echoed the thread paths on the table, connecting the body, its traces and personal expression in one space.

On site

Visitors could wind threads, find cards, stack transparent layers and watch the films. Five demonstration videos explored different directions, including live action, body contours and comic treatments.

Recognition

The project received the course collaboration's sole Best Award and recognition from Chengshi Sports.
